## Evidence tier

**PUBLIC-PRELIMINARY.** Logged-in Upwork job search is not accessible to automated collection. Every attempt to fetch an individual Upwork job-posting URL or the Upwork web-scraping category page returned HTTP 403 Forbidden during this collection pass (see Evidence gaps). Direct fetches of WeWorkRemotely's programming category also returned HTTP 403.

This matrix is therefore built from **publicly visible postings on other freelance marketplaces (PeoplePerHour, Freelancer.com), dated third-party industry/market reports, vendor product pages, and practitioner/procurement commentary** — not from a verified sample of current Upwork job postings. It stands in as the best available public proxy for Upwork-adjacent freelance demand in this category.

It may be upgraded to **VERIFIED-SAMPLE** if the operator supplies a real Upwork posting sample (e.g., logged-in export, screenshots, or pasted posting text) for direct analysis.

## Synthesis

- **Recurring, compliant B2B/B2G contact and opportunity data is the strongest and cleanest demand cluster.** The UK property-leads posting, the SAM.gov Contract Monitor product, and GovWin IQ all target the same underlying buyer need — qualified contact/opportunity lists for outreach or bid pursuit — and two of the three explicitly build on official APIs or explicitly reject automated scraping of restricted sources. *Evidence.*
- **Buyers pay for verified, deduplicated, "ready for outreach" output more than for raw volume.** The UK leads posting explicitly states "not a bulk scraping job" and demands accuracy/relevance methodology; Price2Spy markets "Automatch" and case-study numbers (85% fewer mismatches, 60% less manual work) as its differentiator; BuiltWith enrichment posting asks for "clean, deduplicated" contacts with "validated" fields. *Evidence.*
- **Anti-bot/CAPTCHA-protected and authentication-gated sources are a recurring theme buyers raise, but not a request to defeat them.** The Hungarian insurance-database posting names CAPTCHA as an obstacle without specifying a bypass method, and the 80+-site daily monitoring posting mentions "logging into" sites without clarifying authorization — both are recorded as open compliance questions, not confirmed acceptable work. *Evidence, with interpretation: these should be treated as scope-defining risk signals for the compliance gate rather than templates to replicate.*
- **Recurring monitoring/change-detection is commercially validated at the product level, separate from one-off Upwork gigs.** Price2Spy (competitor pricing), the SAM.gov actor (procurement), and the 80+-site daily-check posting all describe scheduled, repeated runs with diffing/alerting rather than single-delivery datasets — and the Neudata report independently confirms buyer preference is shifting "from static historical datasets toward continuous real-time feeds." *Evidence.*
- **Official APIs are treated as the preferred, lower-risk path when one exists**, and clients/vendors say so directly: the SAM.gov actor uses the free government API "not scraping"; the Google Business Profile guidance uses the official API "no scraping described"; Google Places' "no bulk export allowed" restriction is cited as a boundary to respect. *Evidence.* Where no acceptable API exists (e.g., multi-country school directories, MEB register), postings ask for direct site collection instead — a reasonable but not automatically risk-free substitute. *Interpretation.*
- **Multi-country / multi-format enrichment (institution directories in England, Norway, Slovakia; Turkish MEB schools) is a recognizable recurring pattern**, distinct from single-country contact lists, and demonstrates handling of varied national site structures and languages in one deliverable. *Evidence.*
- **Tooling demand from developer-facing survey data (Apify, PromptCloud, Proxyway) consistently centers on Python, browser automation frameworks (Playwright/Puppeteer), and commercial unblocking/proxy infrastructure**, with blocking cited as the top practical obstacle (68% of surveyed developers) ahead of authentication barriers (32%). *Evidence*, though this is developer self-report data about scraping practice broadly, not proof of Upwork-specific buyer requirements. *Interpretation.*
- **Explicit prohibition of automated methods appears in real postings, not just as a hypothetical risk.** Both the UK leads posting (bans automated LinkedIn scraping) and the Amazon monitoring posting (bans bots/scripts entirely, "manual data collection only") show buyers actively drawing this line themselves, reinforcing that compliant, disclosed, API-first or manually-verified approaches are marketable rather than a competitive disadvantage. *Evidence.*
- **Proof-of-work expectations are unevenly documented across sources.** Only the UK leads posting and the Upwork hiring-guide secondary summary describe concrete proof criteria (portfolio examples, GitHub repos, sourcing/verification methodology, accuracy assurance); most other postings in this sample simply did not have proof requirements captured at the listing-page level. *Evidence of an evidence gap, not evidence of absence.*

## Common failure points and risky-work signals

- **CAPTCHA-protected sources named without a specified bypass method** (Hungarian insurance database posting) — flagged for compliance scrutiny; defeating CAPTCHAs is out of scope under this project's non-negotiable rules and must not be assumed acceptable just because a client posted the job.
- **"Logging into websites daily" framing for an 80+-site monitoring agent** — must be verified as client-authorized-account use before being treated as an approved analog; unauthorized or shared-credential access would violate the project's rules.
- **Deliberate email "decoding" of an obfuscation scheme on a government register (Turkish MEB "CHK algorithm")** — needs confirmation that this is reversing public-facing obfuscation rather than defeating an access control, before being treated as safely in-scope.
- **Automated scraping explicitly banned by name in two real postings** (LinkedIn scraping in the UK leads job; bots/scripts entirely in the Amazon monitoring job) — a direct, dated signal that clients in this space actively police for prohibited automated methods, and that a portfolio project should default to disclosed, permitted-source, API-first collection.
- **BuiltWith-based contact enrichment implies chaining a third-party SaaS platform with a separate contact-matching step** — the data-matching boundary and BuiltWith's own terms were not verified in this pass and would need review before treating this pattern as a safe template.
- **Google Places API's "no bulk export allowed" restriction** is a concrete, documented example of an access boundary that must be respected rather than routed around, even when the underlying data is nominally "public."
